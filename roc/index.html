ROC Curve Lab

A classifier gives every example a score. To get a yes/no answer you pick a threshold τ. Every choice of τ yields a confusion matrix, and from it a pair of rates (FPR, TPR). Sweep τ across all values and those pairs trace out the ROC curve.

Experiment 1

Drag the threshold and watch the point move

The two humps are the classifier's scores for examples that are truly negative (y = 0) and truly positive (y = 1). Everything right of τ gets predicted positive. Drag the black line on the left chart, or drag along the curve on the right.

TP: positive, called positive FN: positive, called negative FP: negative, called positive TN: negative, called negative

Try this: set separation to 0 (the classifier is guessing) and the curve falls onto the diagonal. Then change the share of positives. The humps change size, the counts change, but the ROC curve does not move, because TPR and FPR are each computed within one true class.

Where should τ go? Let the costs decide

From §5.1.2.2: if a false negative costs c times as much as a false positive, predict positive when p(y = 1 | x) ≥ 1/(1 + c). The green marker shows that threshold on both charts.

Experiment 2

Calculate an ROC curve by hand

With a real test set you have a finite list of scored examples. The recipe: sort by score from high to low, start with τ above every score, then lower τ past one example at a time. Each positive you pass moves the curve up by 1/P. Each negative moves it right by 1/N.

Experiment 3

TPR, FPR, TNR, FNR: four rates, two rows

All four rates divide by a row of the confusion matrix, meaning they condition on the true label: p(ŷ | y). Pick a rate to see which cell is on top of the fraction and which row is on the bottom. Precision is included as a contrast because it divides by a column instead.

Example: a screening test applied to 100 people, 20 of whom are truly positive.

How to read the names

The last word before "rate" is what the classifier predicted. The first word says whether that prediction was right. The denominator is everyone who truly belongs to the same class as that cell.

TPRpredicted Positive, correctly. So truly positive. ÷ all truly positive (P).
FNRpredicted Negative, wrongly. So truly positive. ÷ all truly positive (P).
FPRpredicted Positive, wrongly. So truly negative. ÷ all truly negative (N).
TNRpredicted Negative, correctly. So truly negative. ÷ all truly negative (N).

That gives two pairs that always add to 1: TPR + FNR = 1 (the positive row) and FPR + TNR = 1 (the negative row). The ROC curve plots one rate from each row: TPR on the y-axis and FPR on the x-axis. Together they describe the whole matrix.
